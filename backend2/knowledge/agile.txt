Agile methodology works best for:
- rapidly changing requirements
- startup MVPs
- iterative releases
- small cross-functional teams (3-7 members)
- products needing frequent user feedback

Best practices:
- weekly sprints (1-2 week cycles)
- continuous testing and integration
- rapid feedback loops with stakeholders
- daily standups (15 min max)
- sprint retrospectives after every cycle
- user stories over technical specs
- working software over documentation
- velocity tracking per sprint

Common risks:
- scope creep from constant reprioritization
- poor sprint planning leading to burnout
- lack of documentation for handoffs
- technical debt from speed-first mentality
- stakeholder fatigue from frequent demos

Sprint planning heuristics:
- 1 developer = ~20 story points per 2-week sprint
- always reserve 20% capacity for bugs and tech debt
- MVP features should fit in 2-3 sprints max
- never plan more than 80% sprint capacity
- first sprint should produce a deployable skeleton

Team allocation patterns:
- 3-person team: 1 fullstack, 1 backend, 1 frontend
- 5-person team: 2 backend, 2 frontend, 1 DevOps
- always assign 1 person as Scrum Master (can be part-time)

Successful agile startup examples:
- Spotify: squad-based agile with autonomous teams
- Slack: 2-week sprints with continuous deployment
- Airbnb: feature flags + rapid iteration cycles
