Waterfall methodology works best for:
- well-defined, stable requirements
- regulated industries (healthcare, finance, government)
- projects with fixed scope and budget
- hardware-software integration projects
- large teams with clear role separation

Standard waterfall phases:
1. Requirements Gathering (10-15% of timeline)
2. System Design (15-20% of timeline)
3. Implementation/Coding (30-40% of timeline)
4. Testing & QA (15-20% of timeline)
5. Deployment (5-10% of timeline)
6. Maintenance (ongoing)

Best practices:
- comprehensive requirements document before any coding
- formal design reviews at each phase gate
- complete test plans before implementation begins
- change control board for scope modifications
- detailed documentation at every phase
- sign-off required before moving to next phase

Common risks:
- late discovery of requirement misunderstandings
- inflexible to market changes during development
- big-bang integration failures
- long feedback cycles (months before user sees product)
- overengineering due to upfront planning bias

Timeline heuristics:
- 3-person team, 5-week project: requirements (3 days), design (5 days), coding (12 days), testing (4 days), deploy (1 day)
- always add 25% buffer for waterfall projects
- testing phase typically takes 1.5x longer than estimated
- documentation adds ~15% overhead to each phase

When to avoid waterfall:
- startup MVPs with uncertain product-market fit
- projects where requirements will change frequently
- small teams that need flexibility
- consumer-facing apps needing rapid iteration
